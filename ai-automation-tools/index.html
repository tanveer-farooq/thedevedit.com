The 7 best AI automation tools, reviewed

01
Za

Zapier

Best overall

Zapier is an AI automation platform that connects 9,000+ apps and lists Zapier MCP on every plan, Free included (pricing).

9.7/10 Visit Zapier

On paid plans, Zapier says "AI by Zapier is where your agents now run."

Why we picked it

Zapier is the only tool here with full marks on five of our six criteria, losing points only on pricing clarity. Admins get SAML SSO and an audit log from the Team plan, and SCIM on Enterprise.

Who it's for

Business teams that want AI steps across many SaaS apps without hosting anything.

Standout features

  • Your own model: AI by Zapier steps can run on your OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Azure OpenAI or Amazon Bedrock account.
  • Model tiers: each AI step uses Standard, Advanced or Premium models, and Advanced uses 3x tasks, Premium 5x.
  • Human in the Loop: pauses a Zap "so a human can review" before it continues, on Professional and above.
  • Copilot: turns a description into "a basic outline of a trigger and one or more actions."

Integrations

Integrations include Google Sheets, Gmail, Slack, Microsoft Teams, Salesforce and HubSpot (Zapier apps).

Pricing

Free ($0, 100 tasks a month); Professional from $19.99 and Team from $69 a month, billed annually; Enterprise custom (pricing, as of November 2026).

Pros

  • Largest stated app catalogue on this list.
  • No training on workflow data, "on any plan."
  • SOC 2 (Type II) and SOC 3 certified.

Cons

  • Agents is moving into AI by Zapier, billed in Zap tasks.
  • Pay-per-task overage rate "depends on your plan and billing cycle type."
  • Free can't publish a Zap with an AI by Zapier step.

02
Ui

UiPath

Best for enterprise automation

UiPath is one platform for software robots, AI agents and human review, and its free Community plan includes Autopilot.

9.7/10 Visit UiPath

UiPath Autopilot is built to "Translate natural language into workflows, agents, schemas, and automations."

Why we picked it

UiPath ties Zapier at 9.7, with full marks on four of our six criteria. It ranks second on the unrounded total because we found no stated size for its connector catalogue.

Who it's for

Operations and IT teams that automate desktop and legacy apps with robots and want AI agents to hand unclear cases to a person.

Standout features

  • Escalations: when an agent needs help, escalations "are powered by Action apps in Action Center".
  • Agent tools: an agent's tools can run your existing automations, call APIs and work with robots (UiPath docs).
  • MCP Servers: UiPath MCP Servers in Orchestrator expose your automations and agents as tools for MCP clients.
  • Automation Suite: a self-hosted deployment for data that must stay in-house.

Integrations

Integrations include Salesforce, Slack, Microsoft Teams, Google Sheets, ServiceNow and Workday (UiPath Integration Service).

Pricing

Community "Free forever"; Basic from $25 a month (up to 2 robots); Standard and Enterprise via sales, and agent runs consume units (pricing, as of November 2026).

Pros

  • Starts automations from time, queue, event and API triggers.
  • Agents: "We do not use enterprise customers' data to train models."
  • Automation Cloud: SOC 2 Type 2, ISO/IEC 27001, FedRAMP Moderate.

Cons

  • Basic is "Hosted in our European region".
  • Community eligibility is set by "the Community Agreement".
  • By default, Community agents "can only use Bring Your Own models."

n8n

Best for self-hosting

n8n is an AI automation platform whose free Community edition runs on your own server without a license key.

9.6/10 Visit n8n

Its Cloud plans are priced by workflow executions "regardless of complexity", so adding AI steps doesn't raise n8n's own count.

Why we picked it

n8n scores 10 on four of six criteria, including AI and run controls. It pairs 2,000+ listed integrations with HTTP Request and Code nodes for apps outside the catalogue.

Who it's for

Technical teams that can run their own servers and want AI agents inside workflows.

Standout features

  • Tool approvals: n8n can "require human approval before an AI Agent executes a specific tool."
  • AI Workflow Builder: lets you "create, refine, and debug workflows using natural language descriptions of your goals."
  • MCP Client Tool: connects an agent "to any MCP server manually with its connection URL and credentials."
  • Error workflows: a failed run can trigger a separate workflow that starts with the Error Trigger node.

Integrations

Integrations include Google Sheets, Gmail, Slack, Notion, Airtable and HubSpot (n8n integrations).

Pricing

Free self-hosted Community edition; Starter €20, Pro €50 and Business €667 (self-hosted only) a month, billed annually; Enterprise via sales (pricing, as of November 2026).

Pros

  • Downloadable SOC 3 report; SOC 2 report "available to enterprise customers".
  • Project roles on every n8n Cloud plan.
  • Runs agents on local models via the Ollama Chat Model node.

Cons

  • In Community, only the instance owner and creator can access a workflow.
  • SSO only on Business and Enterprise.
  • Sustainable Use License: internal business, non-commercial or personal use only.

Activepieces

Best open-source option

Activepieces is an automation platform with an open-source core under the MIT license and flat monthly prices on paid cloud plans.

9.6/10 Visit Activepieces

The free plan includes MCP and API access.

Why we picked it

Activepieces ties n8n at 9.6 and sits fourth on the unrounded total. It gains on admin, with SSO from Team and SCIM and audit logs on Ultimate, but loses on catalogue size and pricing clarity.

Who it's for

Small and mid-size teams that want predictable costs and the option to self-host.

Standout features

  • AI piece: asks, summarizes or extracts, and Run Agent lets an agent "use tools and work step by step".
  • Build with Chat: you "describe the outcome you want", and it picks the pieces and wires the steps.
  • Your own AI keys: from Plus, connect keys for Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, Mistral AI, AWS Bedrock or OpenAI-compatible endpoints.
  • One credit per run: a run costs one credit however many steps it has.

Integrations

Integrations include Google Sheets, Gmail, Slack, Microsoft Teams, HubSpot and Notion (Activepieces pieces).

Pricing

Free ($0, 1,000 credits a month); Plus $20 and Team $200 a month, flat; Ultimate custom; overage $0.007 a credit (pricing, as of November 2026).

Pros

  • Prompts and runs "never train a model, ours or anyone else's."
  • Holds a SOC 2 Type 2 report.
  • Every published flow keeps a version history.

Cons

  • Catalogue lists 775 pieces, under our 1,000-app mark.
  • Approval piece is listed as "Approval (Legacy)".
  • AI steps cost 2, 10 or 20 credits, by model.

Microsoft Power Automate

Best for Microsoft 365 teams

Microsoft Power Automate is an automation platform whose basic version is free for work or school accounts in a Microsoft Entra tenant.

9.4/10 Visit Microsoft Power Automate

Copilot in cloud flows can create a flow from a natural-language description.

Why we picked it

Power Automate has full marks on run controls and integrations, with more than 1,400 prebuilt, certified connectors. It loses one AI point because we found no MCP support documented for cloud flows.

Who it's for

Organizations on Microsoft 365 that want approvals and AI steps in Outlook, Teams and SharePoint workflows.

Standout features

  • Approvals: "Start and wait for an approval" pauses a flow until people answer in Outlook, Teams or the action center.
  • Agent step: Execute Agent and wait calls a Copilot Studio agent from a cloud flow, standard harness agents only.
  • Your own models: prompts can run on Azure AI Foundry models you connect.
  • Retry policy: retries an action "at either fixed or exponential intervals."

Integrations

Integrations include SharePoint, Microsoft Teams, Excel Online (Business), OneDrive for Business, Microsoft Dataverse and Salesforce (connector reference).

Pricing

Free license; Premium $15 per user and Process $150 per bot a month, paid yearly; pay-as-you-go available (pricing, as of November 2026).

Pros

  • Microsoft doesn't train Copilot on your data "unless you provide consent".
  • Flow and permission changes appear in Microsoft Purview logs.
  • In scope for Microsoft's SOC 2 Type 2 attestation.

Cons

  • Agent steps need Copilot Studio, licensed separately ($200 per 25,000 credits).
  • New customers "must purchase Copilot Credits" for AI prompts.
  • Free license: standard connectors only, and no flow sharing.

Make

Best for visual multi-app scenarios

Make is an AI automation platform where you build workflows as visual scenarios across 3,000+ native apps.

9.3/10 Visit Make

Its assistant Maia "creates scenarios, modifies existing ones, and fixes errors for you using your prompts."

Why we picked it

Make earns full marks on AI and integrations, with Make AI Agents, Make AI Tools and a Make MCP server. It drops points on run controls and admin because we found no approval step or SCIM.

Who it's for

Operations and marketing teams that build branching, multi-app workflows and want a low entry price.

Standout features

  • Error handlers: attach Skip, Retry, Resume, Commit or Rollback to a module; Retry stores incomplete executions.
  • Your own AI provider: paid plans can swap Make's AI Provider for a custom provider connection.
  • Version history: Make keeps each saved scenario version for 60 days.
  • Make Code: adds JavaScript or Python steps on paid plans.

Integrations

Integrations include Google Sheets, Gmail, Slack, Microsoft Teams, Salesforce and HubSpot (Make integrations).

Pricing

Free (1,000 credits a month); Core $9, Pro $16 and Teams $29 a month at 10,000 credits, billed annually; Enterprise custom (Make pricing, as of November 2026).

Pros

  • Lowest listed paid entry on this list, at annual billing.
  • SOC 2 Type II and SOC 3 audits; ISO 27001-certified program.
  • Maia system card: customer prompts and outputs aren't used for training.

Cons

  • Approvals: Make's guide builds them from webhooks and data stores.
  • SSO, audit logs and custom roles are Enterprise-only.
  • Extra credits cost 25% more than plan credits.

Automation Anywhere

Best for RPA teams adding agents

Automation Anywhere is an automation platform whose free Community Edition lets small businesses build AI-powered automations.

9.3/10 Visit Automation Anywhere

Community Edition runs on the Automation 360 cloud and includes Prompt-to-Automate, which creates automations from plain-language descriptions.

Why we picked it

Automation Anywhere has full marks on AI and run controls, including Human in the Loop for AI Agents. It ties Make at 9.3 and ranks seventh on the unrounded total, held back by no published paid prices and no stated connector count.

Who it's for

Enterprises that already run RPA bots and want AI agents working on top of them.

Standout features

  • MCP inbound tools: "enable third-party AI agents to discover, run, and retrieve results from Automation Anywhere automations".
  • AI Agents: tools can include other agents, processes, task bots, API tasks and forms.
  • AI Skills: reusable steps that reach Amazon Bedrock, Azure OpenAI or Google Vertex AI models through Model connections.
  • On-Premises: the Control Room can run on infrastructure you own and manage.

Integrations

Integrations include Microsoft 365 Outlook, Microsoft Teams, SharePoint, Google Sheets, ServiceNow and Slack (Automation 360 packages).

Pricing

Free Community Edition for up to five machines, "No credit card required"; we found no published prices for paid plans (as of November 2026).

Pros

  • "No Customer Data is used to train the models" for generative AI.
  • Cites compliance with SOC 1, SOC 2 and ISO 27001:2022.
  • REST Web Service and Python Script packages for custom steps.

Cons

  • Community Edition excludes firms over 250 users, 250 machines or $5M revenue.
  • AI Agents consume Automation AI Credits, with a base entitlement on Enterprise.
  • Co-Pilot requires an Automator AI Platform Base license.

FAQ

FAQs about AI automation tools

What is the best free AI automation tool?

n8n's Community edition, if you can run a server: n8n's edition comparison lists no execution or workflow cap for it. In the cloud, Activepieces Free gives 1,000 credits a month for up to 5 users, and our free AI tools list goes wider.

Which AI automation tool is easiest for beginners?

Start with Zapier or Make, since both draft a workflow from a plain-language description. Zapier's Copilot works on every plan, while Make's Maia is on paid plans with a 30-day trial on Free, and both are in beta.

What's the difference between an AI automation tool and an AI agent?

An automation tool runs steps you define from a trigger, while an AI agent picks its own steps and tool calls to reach a goal. All seven here can run an agent as a workflow step, with triggers and run logs around it.

Do I need to know how to code?

Not for most workflows, because Zapier, Make, Power Automate and Activepieces are visual builders with prebuilt actions. Code steps help when those fall short, such as n8n's Code node (JavaScript or Python) or UiPath's Invoke Code (VB.NET or C#).

n8n or Zapier?

Pick Zapier for the largest stated catalogue and nothing to host, or n8n to self-host for free or pay per execution in the cloud. Zapier scores 9.7 to n8n's 9.6: n8n loses 0.2 for SCIM we didn't find and gains 0.1 for simpler pricing.
