What each tool does

CrowdStrike Falcon is an endpoint security platform that runs on one agent. Its endpoint page describes a "single lightweight sensor and unified platform."

SentinelOne Singularity is an endpoint and identity security platform from SentinelOne. Its endpoint page describes correlating behavior across endpoints and identities, and its FAQ calls it a single-agent EPP and EDR solution.

Feature by feature

Deployment options

CrowdStrike describes Falcon as one lightweight sensor on a unified platform, with no on-premises option listed on the pages we checked. SentinelOne lists deployment "Across SaaS, on-premises, hybrid, and air-gapped environments" on its endpoint page.

Its FAQ adds "The SentinelOne agent is designed to work online or offline."

Winner: SentinelOne, for regulated or disconnected networks.

Response and recovery

SentinelOne says the platform can "roll back changes to restore systems to a trusted state" on its endpoint page. Its FAQ limits this to Windows.

We found no rollback claim on the CrowdStrike pages we checked.

Winner: SentinelOne, for documented rollback.

AI security analyst

CrowdStrike's Charlotte AI page says "Charlotte AI reasons, decides, and acts at machine speed." SentinelOne's Purple AI page calls Purple AI "an agentic AI security analyst embedded in the Singularity Platform."

Winner: Tie, since both document an agentic analyst.

Pricing and buying

CrowdStrike publishes monthly and annual prices and sells Falcon Go, Pro and Enterprise online. SentinelOne's pricing page states "All purchases will be made through an authorized third-party partner."

Winner: CrowdStrike, for self-serve buying.

Data retention

SentinelOne Singularity Complete lists "14 days of Data Retention" on its pricing page. Singularity Commercial raises that to 90 days.

We did not find per-bundle retention figures on CrowdStrike's pricing page.

Winner: SentinelOne, for stating retention per plan.

Strengths and weaknesses

CrowdStrike Falcon PROS

  • Published monthly and annual prices per device
  • 15-day free trial and 30-day refund
  • Single sensor covers Windows, macOS and Linux

CrowdStrike Falcon CONS

  • Falcon Go caps purchases at 100 devices
  • No on-premises deployment listed on pages checked
  • Retention per bundle not shown on pricing page

SentinelOne PROS

  • SaaS, on-premises, hybrid and air-gapped deployment
  • Windows rollback after ransomware encryption
  • Agent detection runs locally, online or offline

SentinelOne CONS

  • Purchases go through a partner only
  • No free trial listed on pricing page
  • Entry plan costs more than Falcon Go

Who should choose which

  • Choose CrowdStrike Falcon if you run a small or mid-size fleet and want to buy online, pay monthly and start with a trial.
  • Choose SentinelOne if you need on-premises or air-gapped control, Windows rollback or stated 90-day retention.

FAQ

Is CrowdStrike cheaper than SentinelOne? At the entry level, yes. Falcon Go lists $59.99 per device a year, while Singularity Complete lists $179.99 per endpoint a year, though the plans include different features.

Does SentinelOne have a free trial? Its pricing page lists demos, not a free trial. CrowdStrike offers a 15-day free trial covering Falcon Prevent, Device Control and Express Support.

Can SentinelOne run on-premises? Yes. SentinelOne lists SaaS, on-premises, hybrid and air-gapped environments, though its FAQ says it is primarily SaaS based.

Which operating systems do they support? CrowdStrike's pricing FAQ says Falcon supports Windows, macOS and Linux. SentinelOne's FAQ names Linux, macOS and Windows, so check each vendor's support matrix for versions.
