The 7 best free AI tools for 2026

These are the 7 best free AI tools in 2026 for people and teams who want to get work done before they pay: two assistants, a research engine, two coding assistants, an automation platform and a way to run open models on your own computer. Each has a free plan or free edition with no end date. We ranked them by what that free plan documents, from vendor docs and pricing pages, not hands-on tests.

// HOW WE EVALUATED

Seven free AI tools, one rubric, every limit sourced.

On November 14, 2026 we reviewed each tool's official documentation, pricing page, help-center articles, and trust, security or privacy pages, and recorded what the free plan includes, what it caps and what it does with your data. We did not install, run or benchmark any tool, and no vendor saw this article before publication.

How tools were chosen. We built a 10-tool shortlist from the tools that recur in current "free AI tools" roundups and developer-focused free-tool lists, in five jobs: general assistants (ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude, the three that recur most), research (Perplexity), coding (GitHub Copilot and Cursor), automation (Zapier and n8n) and open models (Ollama, plus Hugging Face, which we added as a hosted source of open models). Each had to offer a free plan or free edition with no time limit, public documentation of what it includes, updates in 2026 and availability in the US. We scored all 10. The top scorer in each job made the list, and the last two places went to the next-highest scores, Gemini and Cursor. Hugging Face, Zapier and Claude sit under Also considered with their scores.

How they are ordered. By editorial score, highest first. Ties at one decimal are broken by the unrounded total, then by the data-controls score. Each score is the weighted sum of five criteria, each scored from documented facts:

These are editorial scores from documentation review, not test results, and they are specific to this list because they score only the free plan. A missing element means we did not find it in the vendor's pages, not that the product lacks it. That is why numbers differ from our other lists: Gemini scores 9.4 on our top AI tools list, which also scores capability breadth, integrations and business admin controls, and 8.5 here, where its free plan publishes no numeric limits.

The rubric favors free options that run on your own hardware (n8n and Ollama, at #2 and #3): it treats use on your own machine as uncapped and credits keeping data there. It also deducts for the setup they need. If you can't run software yourself, read the "who it's for" line, not only the number.

ChatGPT

Best overall

ChatGPT's free plan is the only hosted one here that promises unlimited text chats, and it adds limited deep research, uploads, voice chats and Codex, OpenAI's coding agent (ChatGPT pricing).

9.5/10 Visit ChatGPT

You can start without an account: the logged-out page lets you chat and asks you to "Log in to get answers based on saved chats, plus create images and upload files."

WHO IT'S FOR

Anyone who wants one free assistant for writing, questions, files and light coding, with a business plan to move to later.

STANDOUT FEATURE

Plugins on the free plan. The pricing page marks plugins "Yes" for Free, and OpenAI's docs say plugins "bundle capabilities into reusable workflows in ChatGPT and Codex," including MCP servers that connect ChatGPT to outside tools and information.

FREE PLAN

Unlimited text chats "subject to abuse guardrails" and Projects. Uploads, image creation, voice chats, deep research, memory, Codex and ChatGPT Work on desktop are each listed as "Limited", with no published numbers (as of November 2026).

PRICING

As of November 2026, ChatGPT costs $0 (Free), $8/mo (Go), $20/mo (Plus) and from $100/mo (Pro). Business is $20 per user per month billed annually for 2 or more users, and Enterprise is contact sales.

LIMITATIONS

OpenAI says ads "will appear only on the Free and Go plans", and the pricing page says Go "may include ads." On Free, "data sharing is enabled by default," so your chats can train OpenAI's models until you turn off "Improve the model for everyone" (Data controls in ChatGPT).

For a sensitive question, use a Temporary Chat: OpenAI says these chats don't appear in your history, "are not used to improve OpenAI models" and "may be retained for up to 30 days for safety purposes" (Data controls in ChatGPT). ChatGPT runs on the web, on desktop and on iOS and Android (downloads).

For work data, ChatGPT Business adds SAML SSO, MFA and "No training on your business data by default." OpenAI's trust portal lists a SOC 2 Type 2 report.

For other assistants that do ChatGPT's jobs, see our AI tools like ChatGPT list.

n8n

Best for self-hosted automation

Without a license key, n8n runs as the free Community edition, which n8n says "includes almost the complete feature set of n8n."

9.3/10 Visit n8n

You install it on your own server with Docker Compose or a one-line setup script, then build workflows in which an AI agent calls your tools.

WHO IT'S FOR

Operations and engineering teams that can run a server and want AI workflows without a subscription.

STANDOUT FEATURE

Any model, including a local one. The AI Agent node lets you "Connect a chat model and one or more tools, and the agent decides which tools to call to complete a task," and the Ollama Chat Model node points that agent at models on your own hardware.

FREE PLAN

The self-hosted Community edition. n8n's edition comparison lists no execution or workflow cap for it; the execution quotas on n8n's pricing page belong to its paid plans. Not included: SSO, projects, environments, external secrets, log streaming, and workflow and credential sharing.

PRICING

As of November 2026, n8n's paid plans are billed annually in euros: Starter €20/mo (2,500 executions, Cloud), Pro €50/mo (10,000), Business €667/mo (40,000, self-hosted) and Enterprise (contact sales). The Cloud trial needs no credit card.

LIMITATIONS

In the Community edition, "Only the instance owner and the user who creates them can access workflows and credentials," so a team can't share workflows or credentials on it. Self-hosters must set up TLS and handle encrypting data at rest themselves.

Registering the Community edition with your email is free and adds folders, debugging in the editor and custom execution data (n8n editions).

On a self-hosted instance, n8n says workflow data "stays on your infrastructure and we never receive it." Usage telemetry is on by default until you set N8N_DIAGNOSTICS_ENABLED=false (n8n privacy docs), and n8n prunes execution data after 14 days by default (n8n docs).

The MCP Client Tool connects an agent "to any MCP server manually with its connection URL and credentials." n8n's security page says its program "aligns" to SOC 2 and points to a public SOC 3 report.

Ollama

Best for local models

Ollama runs open models on your own computer, and its pricing page says "Running models on your own hardware is always unlimited." The $0 plan also offers cloud models, billed per token, so you pay only when you send work off your machine.

8.9/10 Visit Ollama
WHO IT'S FOR

Developers and privacy-minded teams who want open models on their own hardware and want to call them from their own apps.

STANDOUT FEATURE

A local API that existing tools understand. Ollama's docs say you can "use Ollama's API or compatible OpenAI and Anthropic clients", and its integrations page lists Claude Code, Claude Desktop, ChatGPT Desktop and VS Code.

FREE PLAN

Unlimited local use on macOS, Windows and Linux. The desktop app "supports file drag and drop, making it easier to reason with text or PDFs".

Cloud models are "Pay per token" on the $0 plan and need an Ollama account.

PRICING

As of November 2026, Ollama lists Pay as you go ($0, cloud models billed per token), Pro ($20/mo or $200/yr, with $60 of usage credits a month), Max ($100/mo, $300 of credits), Team ($500/mo in early access, $1,000 of credits shared across the team) and Enterprise (custom).

LIMITATIONS

The free plan includes no hosted compute: cloud models are billed per token from the first request. We found no SSO or compliance certification on Ollama's pages; the early-access Team plan lists "centralized billing and administration".

Ollama says "Your prompts are never stored or trained on", and for its cloud models, "Prompt or response data is never logged or trained on." The app can also send images to models that support them, such as Google DeepMind's Gemma 3 (Ollama blog, July 30, 2025).

Ollama's docs list macOS Sonoma (v14) or newer and Windows 10 22H2 or newer, and warn that models "can be tens to hundreds of GB in size" (macOS, Windows), so check a model's size against your machine before you pull it. To put a local model inside a workflow, pair Ollama with n8n's Ollama Chat Model node (see #2).

GitHub Copilot

Best for coding in your editor

Copilot Free gives you 2,000 code completions a month and agent mode in Visual Studio Code, Visual Studio, Xcode, Eclipse and other editors, at no cost.

8.7/10 Visit GitHub Copilot

Copilot Free publishes a number for its free cap, while Cursor's free plan lists only "Limited agent requests".

WHO IT'S FOR

Individual developers who want AI help inside the editor they already use, with a published monthly cap.

STANDOUT FEATURE

Agent mode with MCP on the free plan. The plans page marks agent mode and "Integrates with MCP servers" as included on Free, so the agent can work on your project and call outside tools from your editor.

The Copilot CLI is included too.

FREE PLAN

"Limited to 2000 completions per month on Copilot Free." Chat and agents draw on an allowance of AI credits with no number published, and models use "Auto model selection only."

PRICING

As of November 2026, per month: Free, Pro ($10), Pro+ ($39), Max ($100), Business ($19 per granted seat) and Enterprise ($39 per granted seat). Paid plans include AI credits, where "1 AI credit = $0.01 USD".

LIMITATIONS

Starting April 24, 2026, interactions on Copilot Free may be used to train AI models until you set "Allow GitHub to use my data for AI model training" to Disabled. Code review and the cloud coding agent need a paid plan.

On Free, Copilot picks the model for you ("Auto model selection only"), and the plans page marks 2 models as included for Free: Anthropic's Claude Haiku 4.5 and OpenAI's GPT-5 mini. We found no Copilot-specific compliance certification on the pages we could read, and no retention setting for individual users.

Copilot Business can be managed through an enterprise account: with managed users, you provision accounts from your identity provider through SCIM and people sign in with single sign-on; with personal accounts, SAML single sign-on is optional (Copilot Business docs).

Gemini

Best for Google account users

Gemini's free plan includes Deep Research, Gemini Live, Canvas, Skills, and image generation and editing (Gemini plans), and you can use some features in the Gemini web app without signing in.

8.5/10 Visit Gemini

Gemini's paid entry, Google AI Plus at $4.99 a month (as of November 2026), is the cheapest upgrade on this list.

WHO IT'S FOR

People with a personal Google account who want free deep research and an assistant that can reach their Google apps and some third-party apps.

STANDOUT FEATURE

Connected Apps. Beyond Google's own apps, Gemini connects to third-party services such as Dropbox, Airtable, Linear and Spotify, most of them for personal Google accounts only; Dropbox, Airtable and Linear are listed as US only, English only, for users 18 or over (Connected Apps table).

FREE PLAN

"Access to our Flash-Lite model," the features above, Gemini Notebook and 15 GB of storage. Usage is listed as "Standard limits" with no numbers, and "Your limit refreshes every 5 hours until you reach your weekly limit."

The free context window is 32k tokens.

PRICING

As of November 2026: Free ($0), Google AI Plus ($4.99/mo), Google AI Pro ($19.99/mo) and Google AI Ultra (from $99.99/mo), and "You can extend your limits by purchasing AI credits." Gemini Enterprise starts at $21 per seat per month (Business edition), with no billing term stated.

LIMITATIONS

For free users, Google says features such as Deep Research "may be unavailable during periods of high demand" (limits page). With Keep Activity on, Google uses your activity "to provide, develop, and improve its services (including training generative AI models)," and "A subset of chats are reviewed by human reviewers" (Gemini Apps Privacy Hub).

You can turn Keep Activity off, and Google states that "Temporary chats are not used to train Google's AI models." Auto-delete can be changed "from the default of 18 months to 3 months, 36 months, or indefinite" (Privacy Hub).

For a team, Gemini Enterprise works with identity providers that support OIDC or SAML 2.0 (Google Cloud docs), and Google says Gemini has certifications including ISO 42001, BSI C5 and FedRAMP High (Workspace AI privacy).

Cursor

Best for coding in an AI editor

Cursor's pricing page marks Cursor Agent as included on the free plan, and Cursor says the Agent "can complete complex coding tasks independently, run terminal commands, and edit code."

8.5/10 Visit Cursor

You use it in Cursor's own editor, on what Cursor calls Cursor models (Grok and Composer). Cursor's help center says Cursor "is part of The X Subscription, one subscription for Grok, Cursor, and X," and new customers pick their plan there (Cursor pricing help).

WHO IT'S FOR

Developers willing to work in Cursor's editor to get a coding agent at no cost, with an $8 plan to move to.

STANDOUT FEATURE

Privacy Mode on the free plan. Cursor says Privacy Mode "is available to anyone (free or Pro)" and "When enabled, we will not train on your data."

FREE PLAN

"Limited agent requests" and "Limited Chat requests," with no published numbers. Cursor's help center says you "can use Agent, Chat, and Tab completions with Cursor models (Grok and Composer)" (Cursor pricing help).

Cloud agents and automations, and AI code reviews via Bugbot, are not on Free (as of November 2026).

PRICING

As of November 2026, Cursor's monthly prices are Free ($0), Plus ($8), Premium ($30), Super ($100), Ultra ($200), Teams ($30+ per user) and Enterprise (custom). Plus is limited to Grok models ("Only Grok models are available on this plan"), and Cursor says "Third Party model usage in Cursor is not included in the monthly prices shown and must be purchased separately."

LIMITATIONS

Free has no published allowance, and cloud agents and Bugbot code reviews start at $30 a month (Premium). Cursor states a Privacy Mode default only for teams ("For teams, Privacy Mode is enabled by default for all team members"), so on a free account you turn it on in settings.

Cursor's docs say MCP "enables Cursor to connect to external tools and data sources," and the Cursor CLI "lets you interact with AI agents directly from your terminal to write, review, and modify code." Cursor's security page says "Cursor holds AIUC-1, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, and ISO/IEC 42001:2023 certifications, along with a SOC 2 Type II attestation."

For a team, Teams adds "SAML/OIDC SSO" and "Custom Chat data retention" (Cursor pricing).

Best for cited research

Perplexity is the research pick: its free plan puts "Citations in every answer", and you can search without an account. Logged-out sessions "expire after 14 days and are not recoverable," so sign in for anything you want to keep.

8.3/10 Visit Perplexity
WHO IT'S FOR

Analysts, students and anyone who needs a few answers a day that they can check against the sources.

STANDOUT FEATURE

Incognito sessions, which Perplexity describes as "private conversations that expire after 24 hours", for queries you don't need to keep.

FREE PLAN

"Good for limited daily usage": 3 Pro Searches a day and 1 Research query a month, limited file uploads and "Access to basic AI models." App connectors, model selection and Computer credits are not on Free (pricing).

PRICING

As of November 2026, Pro is $20/mo or $200/yr ($17/mo billed annually on the pricing page), Max $200/mo or $2,000/yr, Enterprise Pro $40 per seat per month and Enterprise Max $325 per seat per month (Perplexity help center).

LIMITATIONS

"AI Data Retention is enabled by default" for Free, Pro and Max users, so your data can be used for AI training until you toggle it off in Preferences. Basic searches are capped, with no published number.

For teams, Perplexity Enterprise lists SSO and states: "We never train our LLMs on enterprise customer's data." Perplexity's security page says it is "SOC 2 Type 2 certified by independent auditors."

// HOW TO CHOOSE

Four questions, in this order.

1

Which job comes first?

For everyday writing and questions, start with ChatGPT or Gemini; for answers with sources, Perplexity. For code, take GitHub Copilot Free in the editor you already use, or Cursor if you'll work in Cursor's own editor; for workflows, n8n; for open models, Ollama on your machine.

2

Will work data go in?

ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, GitHub Copilot and Cursor each have a setting that decides whether free-plan content can be used for training, so turn it off first, or ask for a business plan with SSO (see the table). With n8n and Ollama on your own hardware, the data stays with you unless you send it to a cloud model.

3

Can you run software yourself?

If yes, Ollama says local use "is always unlimited," and n8n lists no execution cap for its Community edition, but you maintain the machine. If no, ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity work in the browser with nothing to install.

4

What does the first paid step cost?

As of November 2026: Gemini's Google AI Plus $4.99/mo, ChatGPT Go $8, Cursor Plus $8, GitHub Copilot Pro $10, Perplexity Pro $17 (annual) or $20, n8n Starter €20 (annual, Cloud) and Ollama Pro $20. Only ChatGPT's pricing page says its entry plan "may include ads."

// FAQ

Questions people actually ask

Are free AI tools really free, or is it a trial?

Every tool on this list has a free plan or free edition with no end date. Trials are separate offers: n8n's Cloud trial needs no credit card, and verified teachers and maintainers of popular open-source projects may qualify for free Copilot Pro (Copilot plans docs). Usage can still cost money on a free account, for example cloud models on Ollama's $0 plan.

Can I use free AI tools for work?

Yes, within each vendor's terms. Copilot Free is only for people who don't get Copilot through an organization or enterprise (Copilot plans docs), and n8n's Sustainable Use License allows use "only for your own internal business purposes or for non-commercial or personal use". For company-wide use, we found business plans with SSO for every tool here except Ollama.

Can I run AI models on my own computer for free?

Yes, if your computer can hold the model. Ollama's cloud docs explain how to keep inference on your own hardware and turn off its cloud features, and Hugging Face models can be cloned locally and run with its libraries. Ollama's docs list the supported systems (macOS Sonoma or newer, Windows 10 22H2 or newer) and warn that models can take tens to hundreds of GB of disk space, so start with a small model.

What happened to Gemini Code Assist's free tier?

Google ended it. "Starting June 18, 2026, Gemini Code Assist IDE extensions stopped serving requests for the Gemini Code Assist for individuals, Google AI Pro, and Google AI Ultra tiers," and the change also applies to Gemini CLI. Google points consumer users to its Antigravity products, so lists that still quote a free Gemini Code Assist allowance are out of date.

GitHub Copilot Free or Cursor's free plan?

Copilot Free if you want to stay in your current editor and know your cap: 2,000 completions a month, with agent mode and MCP. Cursor's free plan runs inside Cursor's own app, uses Cursor's Grok and Composer models, and lists only "Limited" usage. Both cheapest paid plans cost $10 or less a month as of November 2026 (Copilot Pro $10, Cursor Plus $8).
