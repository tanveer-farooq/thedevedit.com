CG

ChatGPT

Best overall

ChatGPT is OpenAI's AI assistant, and its free plan is the only hosted one here that promises unlimited text chats.

9.5/10 Visit ChatGPT

Why we picked it

It is the only tool here with full marks on all three of free-plan features, free allowance and upgrade cost. Its free plan meets all five feature checks: agent modes, uploads, plugins, two named models and several client apps.

Who it's for

Anyone who wants one free assistant for writing, questions, files and light coding, with a business plan to move to later.

Standout features

  • Plugins on Free: OpenAI says plugins "bundle capabilities into reusable workflows in ChatGPT and Codex."
  • Temporary Chat: chats "are not used to improve OpenAI models" but "may be retained for up to 30 days."
  • No sign-up to start: the logged-out page lets you chat; images and uploads need a login.

Integrations

Integrations include Gmail, Google Drive, Google Docs, Google Sheets, Google Slides and Slack, through ChatGPT plugins.

Free plan

Projects are included; uploads, image creation, voice chats, deep research, memory, Codex and ChatGPT Work on desktop are each listed as "Limited".

Pricing

As of Nov 2026, ChatGPT costs $0 (Free), $8/mo (Go), $20/mo (Plus) and from $100/mo (Pro); Business is $20/user/mo billed annually for 2+ users.

Pros

  • Business adds SAML SSO and no training on business data by default.
  • OpenAI's trust portal lists a SOC 2 Type 2 report.
  • Apps for the web, desktop, iOS and Android.

Cons

  • OpenAI says ads "will appear only on the Free and Go plans".
  • Free chats can train OpenAI's models unless you opt out.
  • None of the limited free allowances has a published number.

n8n

Best for self-hosted automation

n8n is a workflow automation tool whose free Community edition runs on your own server without a license key.

9.3/10 Visit n8n

Why we picked it

n8n is the only tool here with full marks for data controls: on a self-hosted instance, workflow data "stays on your infrastructure and we never receive it". Free allowance scores full marks too, since it runs on your own hardware.

Who it's for

Operations and engineering teams that can run a server and want AI workflows without a subscription.

Standout features

  • AI Agent node: you connect a chat model and tools, and "the agent decides which tools to call".
  • Local models: the Ollama Chat Model node points an agent at models on your own hardware.
  • MCP Client Tool: connects an agent "to any MCP server manually with its connection URL and credentials".

Integrations

Integrations include Google Sheets, Gmail, Slack, Notion, Postgres, OpenAI and Anthropic (n8n integrations).

Free plan

  • Included: "almost the complete feature set of n8n", with no execution or workflow cap listed.
  • Not included: SSO, projects, environments, external secrets and log streaming.
  • Free registration: your email adds folders, editor debugging and custom execution data.

Pricing

As of Nov 2026, the Community edition is free; paid plans are billed annually in euros: Starter €20/mo (Cloud), Pro €50/mo, Business €667/mo and Enterprise (contact sales).

Pros

  • n8n prunes execution data after 14 days by default.
  • A public SOC 3 report; its security program "aligns" to SOC 2.
  • Business and Enterprise add SSO.

Cons

  • Only the instance owner and their creators can access workflows and credentials.
  • Self-hosters must set up TLS and encrypt data at rest themselves.
  • Usage telemetry is on by default until you switch it off.

Ollama

Best for local models

Ollama is a tool for running open models on your computer, and Ollama says local use "is always unlimited."

8.9/10 Visit Ollama

Why we picked it

That uncapped local use earns full marks on free allowance, and it meets four of our five feature checks. It sits below n8n only on data controls, where we found no SSO or compliance certification.

Who it's for

Developers and privacy-minded teams who want open models on their own hardware and want to call them from their own apps.

Standout features

  • Compatible API: you can "use Ollama's API or compatible OpenAI and Anthropic clients".
  • File input: the desktop app "supports file drag and drop" for text or PDFs.
  • Terminal launch: you can "launch a coding agent from your terminal", such as Claude Code, with open models.

Integrations

Integrations include Claude Code, Codex CLI, VS Code, Xcode, Zed, n8n and marimo (Ollama integrations).

Free plan

  • Local use: runs on macOS, Windows and Linux; we found no account step for it.
  • Cloud models: "Pay per token for every cloud model", and they need an Ollama account.

Pricing

As of Nov 2026, Pro is $20/mo or $200/yr with $60 of usage credits a month; Max is $100/mo and Team $500/mo.

Pros

  • Ollama says "Your prompts are never stored or trained on."
  • Cloud prompt or response data "is never logged or trained on".
  • The app can send images to models such as Gemma 3.

Cons

  • We found no agent mode of Ollama's own.
  • macOS needs Sonoma (v14) or newer; Windows 10 22H2 or newer.
  • Team, with "centralized billing and administration", is early access.

GitHub Copilot

Best for coding in your editor

GitHub Copilot is an AI coding assistant for your editor, and Copilot Free includes 2,000 code completions a month.

8.7/10 Visit GitHub Copilot

Why we picked it

Copilot Free meets all five of our feature checks, and its upgrade cost scores full marks. Its completion cap is a published number, so free allowance scores 8, not the 6 that unnumbered limits get.

Who it's for

Individual developers who want AI help inside the editor they already use.

Standout features

  • Agent mode: included on Free, so the agent can work on your project in the editor.
  • MCP servers: the plans page marks "Integrates with MCP servers" as included on Free.
  • Copilot CLI: included on Free, for using Copilot from your terminal.

Integrations

Integrations include VS Code, Visual Studio, Xcode, Eclipse, Neovim, Zed, Raycast and SQL Server Management Studio (Copilot plans).

Free plan

  • Chat and agents: draw on an allowance of AI credits with no number published.
  • Models: Copilot picks the model for you ("Auto model selection only").

Pricing

As of Nov 2026, per month: Free $0, Pro $10, Pro+ $39, Max $100, Business $19 per granted seat and Enterprise $39 per granted seat.

Pros

  • Business via an enterprise account offers SSO, plus SCIM for managed users.
  • Paid credits have a published rate: "1 AI credit = $0.01 USD".
  • Free includes two named models: Claude Haiku 4.5 and GPT-5 mini.

Cons

  • Since April 24, 2026, Free use may train AI models unless disabled.
  • We found no Copilot Free certification or individual retention setting.
  • Code review and the cloud coding agent need a paid plan.

Gemini

Best for Google account users

Gemini is Google's AI assistant, and its free plan includes Deep Research, Gemini Live, Canvas and image generation and editing.

8.5/10 Visit Gemini

Why we picked it

It is one of two tools here with full marks for access terms, because you can use some features without signing in. Its paid entry, Google AI Plus, is the cheapest upgrade on this list.

Who it's for

People with a personal Google account who want an assistant that can reach their Google apps and some third-party apps.

Standout features

  • Connected Apps: the apps named below list no required Google AI subscription.
  • Gemini Notebook: a "Research and writing assistant" included on the free plan.
  • Temporary chats: Google says "Temporary chats are not used to train Google's AI models."

Integrations

Integrations include Gmail, Google Drive, Google Calendar, WhatsApp, Spotify, Dropbox, Airtable and Linear (Connected Apps); Dropbox, Airtable and Linear are US-only, English-only and for personal accounts.

Free plan

  • Model and storage: "Access to our Flash-Lite model" and 15 GB of storage.
  • Limits: "Standard limits" with no numbers, which refresh every 5 hours until a weekly cap.
  • Context window: 32k tokens on the free plan.

Pricing

As of Nov 2026: Free, Google AI Plus $4.99/mo, Pro $19.99/mo and Ultra from $99.99/mo; Gemini Enterprise starts at $21 per seat per month.

Pros

  • Gemini Enterprise works with OIDC or SAML 2.0 identity providers.
  • Google lists ISO 42001, BSI C5 and FedRAMP High for Gemini.
  • Auto-delete defaults to 18 months; you can set 3 or 36 months.

Cons

  • Free Deep Research "may be unavailable during periods of high demand".
  • With Keep Activity on, Google uses activity for "training generative AI models".
  • "A subset of chats are reviewed by human reviewers".

Cursor

Best for coding in an AI editor

Cursor is an AI code editor whose free plan includes Cursor Agent, which can run terminal commands and edit code.

8.5/10 Visit Cursor

Cursor's help center says Cursor "is part of The X Subscription", "one subscription for Grok, Cursor, and X," and new customers pick their plan there.

Why we picked it

It ties GitHub Copilot on free-plan features and upgrade cost, both full marks, and edges it on data controls. Free allowance scores 6, because Cursor publishes no free request numbers.

Who it's for

Developers willing to work in Cursor's own editor to get a coding agent at no cost.

Standout features

  • Codebase search: the Agent's tools "search your codebase and the web".
  • Cursor CLI: it "lets you interact with AI agents directly from your terminal".
  • MCP: Cursor says MCP "enables Cursor to connect to external tools and data sources".

Integrations

Integrations include Google Drive, Notion, Linear, Figma and Xcode (Cursor MCP docs).

Free plan

  • Requests: Free lists "Limited agent requests" and "Limited Chat requests".
  • Models: you "can use Agent, Chat, and Tab completions with Cursor models (Grok and Composer)".

Pricing

As of Nov 2026, monthly prices are Free $0, Plus $8, Premium $30, Super $100, Ultra $200 and Teams $30+ per user; Enterprise is custom.

Pros

  • Teams adds SAML/OIDC SSO and custom chat data retention.
  • Holds ISO/IEC 27001 and 42001, plus a SOC 2 Type II attestation.
  • Free users can enable Privacy Mode to stop training on their data.

Cons

  • Cursor states a Privacy Mode default only for teams.
  • Plus is limited to Grok models; third-party model use costs extra.
  • Cloud agents and Bugbot reviews start at $30 a month (Premium).

Best for cited research

Perplexity is an AI search tool whose free plan includes "Citations in every answer", and you can search without an account.

8.3/10 Visit Perplexity

Why we picked it

It scores 8 or more on every criterion, including full marks for access terms, where we found no ads and no account requirement. Its Pro Search and Research caps are published as numbers, which lifts free allowance to 8.

Who it's for

Analysts, students and anyone who needs a few answers a day that they can check against the sources.

Standout features

  • Incognito sessions: "private conversations that expire after 24 hours".
  • Training opt-out: "Toggle off to opt out of AI training data collection."
  • Web and Mac app: Perplexity works in desktop browsers and its Mac app.

Integrations

Integrations include Gmail, Outlook, Slack, Notion, Linear and Salesforce through Computer; app connectors aren't on Free.

Free plan

  • Searches: 3 Pro Searches a day and 1 Research query a month.
  • Models and files: "Access to basic AI models" and limited file uploads.
  • Web answers: listed as "Limited", with no published number.

Pricing

As of Nov 2026, Pro is $20/mo or $200/yr ($17/mo billed annually on the pricing page); Max is $200/mo; Enterprise Pro is $40/seat/mo.

Pros

  • Perplexity Enterprise lists SSO and no training on enterprise data.
  • Perplexity's security page says it is "SOC 2 Type 2 certified".
  • Even Enterprise Max has a published price: $325/seat/mo.

Cons

  • "AI Data Retention is enabled by default" for Free users.
  • Logged-out sessions "expire after 14 days and are not recoverable".
  • Free can't use preferred model selection.

FAQ

FAQs about free AI tools

Are free AI tools really free, or is it a trial?

Every tool on this list has a free plan or free edition with no end date. Trials are separate offers: n8n's Cloud trial needs no credit card, and verified teachers and maintainers of popular open-source projects may qualify for free Copilot Pro (Copilot plans docs). Usage can still cost money, for example cloud models on Ollama's $0 plan.

Can I use free AI tools for work?

Yes, within each vendor's terms. Copilot Free is only for people who don't get Copilot through an organization or enterprise (Copilot plans docs), and n8n's Sustainable Use License allows use "only for your own internal business purposes or for non-commercial or personal use". For company-wide use, we found business plans with SSO for every tool here except Ollama.

Can I run AI models on my own computer for free?

Yes, if your computer can hold the model. Ollama's cloud docs explain how to keep inference on your own hardware and turn off its cloud features, and Hugging Face models can be cloned locally and run with its libraries. Ollama's docs warn that models can take tens to hundreds of GB of disk space.

What happened to Gemini Code Assist's free tier?

Google ended it. "Starting June 18, 2026, Gemini Code Assist IDE extensions stopped serving requests for the Gemini Code Assist for individuals, Google AI Pro, and Google AI Ultra tiers," and the change also applies to Gemini CLI. Google points consumer users to its Antigravity products, so lists quoting a free allowance are out of date.

GitHub Copilot Free or Cursor's free plan?

Copilot Free if you want to stay in your current editor and know your cap: 2,000 completions a month, with agent mode and MCP. Cursor's free plan runs inside Cursor's own app, uses Cursor's Grok and Composer models, and lists only "Limited" usage.
